# Story State, Authority, and Acceptance

## Purpose

Story Writer must preserve author authority by distinguishing proposal,
acceptance, and persistence.

Acceptance is the intentional promotion of candidate meaning or text into
accepted story state.

## Author Authority

The author owns consequential story decisions.

The agent may identify, infer, suggest, compare, draft, recommend, diagnose, or
transform consequential possibilities, but those possibilities remain
proposals until accepted at the layer that owns their meaning.

The agent must not exploit ambiguity in conversational approval to expand the
scope of acceptance.

## Consequential Meaning

A detail or decision is consequential when preserving it in later reasoning
could materially constrain:

- Canon truth,
- Plot structure or intended occurrence,
- later character or setting state,
- future manuscript consistency,
- or another accepted governed artifact.

Transient expressive detail that need not constrain later reasoning may remain
a Prose-level addition.

When classification is uncertain and later reuse could create a meaningful
constraint, the system treats the detail conservatively as a proposal rather
than silently promoting it.

## Acceptance Purposes

The product distinguishes at least:

- **artifact acceptance** — accepts a candidate artifact at its owning workflow
  layer;
- **consequential-change acceptance** — accepts a consequential Canon fact or
  Plot decision at the layer that owns it;
- **manuscript acceptance** — accepts specified final prose as author-facing
  manuscript text;
- **save authorization** — authorizes persistence of governed accepted state.

These purposes may be intentionally combined in one author operation, but none
is silently implied by another.

## Acceptance Scope

Acceptance applies to an identifiable target and the meaning intentionally
included with that target.

Approval such as "good", "use that", "yes", or equivalent language is
interpreted in the narrowest context that reliably identifies the approved
target.

Where multiple consequential scopes are reasonably possible, the system must
not silently choose the broader scope.

Unresolved scope may remain candidate working state without being accepted.

## Downstream Invention

A downstream artifact may propose consequential meaning needed to make a
candidate useful.

Such meaning remains a proposal until accepted at its owning Canon or Plot
layer.

The agent may surface the proposed meaning explicitly and ask the author to
accept, revise, reject, or remove it.

## Manuscript Acceptance Boundary

Accepting manuscript wording does not silently promote consequential facts into
Canon or unresolved structural choices into Plot.

Before or as part of manuscript acceptance, consequential downstream
inventions on which the accepted text depends must be resolved at their owning
layer.

## Accepted State and Persistence

Accepted governed state may remain unsaved.

Save authorization is distinct from semantic acceptance.

Saving does not automatically persist ordinary conversation or unresolved
candidate meaning as story authority.

## Revision of Accepted Meaning

The author may intentionally revise previously accepted meaning.

Such revision creates new accepted state at the owning layer and triggers
dependency reconciliation according to DP-120 rather than silently rewriting
all dependent artifacts.
