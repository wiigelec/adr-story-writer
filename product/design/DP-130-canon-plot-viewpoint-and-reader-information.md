# Canon, Plot, Viewpoint, and Reader Information

## Purpose

Story Writer separates omniscient story reality from intentional narrative
presentation.

Canon owns what is true in the story world.

Plot owns how accepted or explicitly declared candidate Canon is selected,
ordered, withheld, revealed, emphasized, and made available to the reader.

This separation lets the Product preserve hidden truth, character knowledge,
dramatic irony, delayed revelation, nonchronological presentation, and
multi-session continuity without forcing generated prose to reconstruct those
distinctions.

## Canon Authority

Canon is the author-facing model of story-world reality.

Accepted Canon meaning is true for governed story reasoning independently of
whether it:

- appears in Plot;
- is known by a character;
- is revealed to the reader;
- is realized in Prose;
- appears in accepted Manuscript;
- occurs on-page or off-page;
- occurs before or after the current drafting position;
- or remains intentionally concealed.

Canon may therefore be materially richer than Plot and Manuscript.

Candidate Canon proposals may be used under DP-110 and DP-120 without becoming
accepted truth.

## Canon Boundary-Critical Scope

For the Canon/Plot boundary, Canon must be able to represent at least:

- chronology and occurrence;
- relationships and state;
- causality and consequence;
- accepted future occurrence;
- character or group knowledge, belief, misunderstanding, and memory;
- hidden causes and unrevealed truth;
- and intentionally unresolved story truth.

Detailed Canon domains, state, events, and temporal semantics are defined by
DP-140.

Author-facing Canon views may organize the same underlying truth in different
ways, but presentation through several views must not create competing
authorities.

## Objective Truth, Knowledge, and Belief

Canon distinguishes objective story truth from epistemic state where that
difference matters.

The Product must be able to distinguish at least:

- what is objectively true in the story world;
- what a character knows;
- what a character believes;
- what a character misunderstands;
- what a group, institution, or culture knows or believes;
- and truth that the author intentionally leaves unresolved or ambiguous.

A false belief may be canonically true as a fact about the believer without
making the believed proposition objectively true.

A character's discovery, forgetting, deception, reinterpretation, or learning
may change Canon epistemic state.

Reader information is not character knowledge and is not objective Canon truth.

## Accepted Future Meaning

Chronological futurity does not make accepted story meaning non-canonical.

An occurrence or state accepted by the author as part of story reality may be
Canon even when it is future relative to the current drafting position,
unrevealed, absent from current Plot, or not yet realized in Prose.

A proposed future occurrence remains candidate Canon until accepted.

Plot and downstream production-control work may depend on such candidate Canon
when it is explicitly declared as a working assumption.

## Plot Authority

Plot is the governed dramatic projection and presentation of Canon.

Plot does not decide whether accepted Canon is true.

Plot decides how story reality is intentionally encountered as narrative.

Plot may govern:

- which Canon material is selected for presentation;
- what is omitted, summarized, implied, dramatized, revisited, or juxtaposed;
- narrative and dramatic order;
- scene or other presentation-unit selection and boundaries;
- viewpoint and focalization;
- information access;
- revelation and concealment;
- dramatic irony;
- delayed explanation;
- misdirection;
- reinterpretation;
- repetition and emphasis;
- and author-declared reader-information intent.

The same Canon may support materially different accepted Plots.

Changing Plot does not inherently change Canon.

## Canon Chronology and Plot Order

World chronology and narrative presentation order are distinct.

Plot may:

- present consequences before causes;
- reveal earlier occurrence later;
- omit an event while presenting its effects;
- revisit one accepted occurrence from several viewpoints;
- distribute one occurrence across several narrative units;
- summarize a long interval;
- dramatize only selected moments;
- or reorder material for dramatic effect.

Plot order must therefore not be treated as the sole representation of Canon
chronology.

Likewise, authoring order is neither story chronology nor authority order and
is not necessarily Plot order.

Material authored or accepted later may describe earlier Canon occurrence, and
accepted future Canon may exist before any Plot unit realizes it.

## Viewpoint Ownership

Viewpoint crosses Plot and Prose but has different meaning at each surface.

Plot owns the **information-access decision**:

- whose perspective governs access;
- what that perspective can perceive or know;
- what information is available or unavailable within the governed scope;
- whether access shifts within or between narrative units;
- and what dramatic restrictions follow from that choice.

Viewpoint access may be governed at whatever narrative scope is semantically
useful. Product Design does not require one fixed scene-, chapter-, or
character-level granularity.

Prose owns the **linguistic realization** of that viewpoint:

- diction;
- sentence construction;
- psychic distance;
- interior rendering;
- sensory phrasing;
- attribution;
- and other textual techniques.

A viewpoint decision must not be inferred as accepted Plot meaning merely
because generated wording happens to sound close, distant, omniscient, or
character-bound.

Likewise, changing linguistic viewpoint technique does not inherently change
the accepted information-access decision.

## Focalization and Information Access

Focalization is a Plot constraint on what information can be presented through
a narrative unit.

A focal character may know less than Canon.

The reader may know more than the focal character through prior Plot
presentation.

The author may know more than both.

The Product must preserve these distinctions so generation does not leak
omniscient Canon merely because the information exists in the Dataset.

Where Plot restricts information access, a generation context must not treat
inaccessible Canon as narratively available merely because it was retrieved for
validation or author reasoning.

Detailed context-projection rules are defined by DP-150.

## Reader-Information Intent

Plot owns governed author intent about what information the narrative should
present, withhold, emphasize, suggest, or position for later reinterpretation.

Reader-information intent governs intended presentation conditions and effects.
It does not guarantee what any actual reader will notice, infer, believe,
remember, or understand.

The Product distinguishes:

- Canon truth;
- character or group knowledge and belief;
- governed reader-information intent;
- reader-facing text actually present in Manuscript;
- and diagnostic inference about what an actual or simulated reader may infer.

These are related but not interchangeable.

Reader-information intent may include:

- reveal;
- concealment;
- partial reveal;
- misdirection;
- ambiguity;
- dramatic irony;
- delayed explanation;
- reminder;
- recontextualization;
- or intentionally unresolved interpretation.

## Reader Knowledge Is Not Automatically Authoritative State

The Product must not automatically treat an inferred model of "what the reader
knows" as accepted story authority.

Actual readers may miss, misread, infer, remember, or forget information in
ways the Product cannot make authoritative merely by prediction.

Diagnostic reader inference may be useful during review.

It may show that realized text fails to produce the intended information effect.

Such diagnosis does not itself change accepted Plot.

If the author changes the intended reader-information progression in response,
that change is a Plot proposal subject to DP-110.

## Reveal and Concealment

Where information timing materially affects narrative meaning, reveal and
concealment are governed Plot concerns rather than incidental prose effects.

Plot may intentionally:

- reveal accepted Canon directly;
- reveal only part of it;
- conceal it;
- imply it;
- present a mistaken interpretation;
- delay explanation;
- expose contradiction;
- repeat or reinforce it;
- or later recontextualize earlier material.

Generated text must not independently introduce a consequential reveal that
Plot has not authorized.

Similarly, generation must not disclose hidden Canon merely because the model
has access to it.

## Discovery and Revelation Are Distinct

A character discovery and a reader revelation may occur together, but they are
different governed meanings.

When a character learns something, Canon epistemic state changes.

When Plot presents that learning or otherwise exposes the information to the
reader, reader-information intent is realized.

A character may learn something off-page without the reader learning it at the
same time.

The reader may learn something that the focal character does not know.

The Product must not collapse those cases.

## Deliberate Ambiguity

Canon may contain unresolved truth when the author intentionally chooses not to
establish one objective answer.

Plot may also intentionally preserve ambiguity even when Canon contains a
definite hidden truth.

These cases are distinct.

**Canon ambiguity** means the story reality itself remains unresolved at the
governed scope.

**Plot ambiguity** means presentation intentionally prevents or delays a
definite reader-facing conclusion.

The Product must not resolve either form of ambiguity merely for schema,
validation, or generation convenience.

## Plot Refinement

Plot may be progressively refined through Synopsis, Outline, Sequence, scene
plans, viewpoint plans, reveal structures, or other production-control
artifacts.

Those structures do not become additional semantic authority surfaces.

Refinement must preserve accepted Plot meaning unless the author intentionally
revises it.

Plot should be refined far enough that downstream Prose generation does not
need to invent consequential dramatic structure, viewpoint access, or reveal
timing.

Generation readiness remains governed by DP-120.

## Canon and Plot Candidate Work

Candidate Plot may depend on accepted Canon, candidate Canon, or both.

Candidate downstream work may explore alternative presentations of the same
Canon or alternative consequences of a candidate Canon proposal.

Candidate assumptions must remain declared according to DP-120.

Accepted Plot may retain unresolved Canon only when the accepted Plot scope does
not materially depend on choosing one unresolved Canon interpretation as true.

Where accepted Plot materially depends on candidate Canon, that dependency must
satisfy DP-110 acceptance closure. Accepting Plot never silently accepts the
candidate Canon.

## Downstream Discovery

Plot development or Prose realization may expose missing, contradictory, or
improved Canon meaning.

Such discovery becomes a Canon proposal.

Prose realization may likewise expose a need to change Plot viewpoint, reveal
timing, selection, or dramatic structure.

Such discovery becomes a Plot proposal.

Downstream appearance does not grant upstream authority.

The lowest-layer revision principle in DP-100 applies: upstream meaning changes
only when the downstream problem cannot be solved while preserving it.

## Validation Relationship

Semantic review must be able to detect material violations of this boundary,
including Canon contradiction, epistemic leakage, unauthorized reveal,
viewpoint-access violation, and confusion between chronology and Plot order.

Validation diagnoses alignment.

It does not create Canon, choose Plot, or establish actual reader knowledge.

Detailed validation behavior is defined by DP-180.

## Revision and Reconciliation

Accepted Canon revision may materially affect Plot, production-control
artifacts, Prose, and Manuscript.

Accepted Plot revision may materially affect production-control artifacts,
Prose, and Manuscript without changing Canon.

Changes to character knowledge or belief are Canon changes even when motivated
by Plot needs.

Changes to reader-information intent are Plot changes even when motivated by
Prose review.

Dependency, impact analysis, and reconciliation follow DP-120.

## Planning Boundary

This Design defines the semantic boundary between Canon and Plot, including
viewpoint information access and reader-information intent.

DP-140 defines detailed Canon state/event/time semantics; DP-150 defines Prose
viewpoint realization and generation-package construction; DP-160 defines
persistence; DP-180 defines validation behavior; and DP-190 defines generated
views.

Planning may choose storage partitions, schemas, identifiers, indexes, reveal
representations, knowledge-state representations, viewpoint structures, and
other technical realization.

Implementation convenience does not override Canon/Plot ownership or permit
hidden truth to leak across governed information boundaries.
