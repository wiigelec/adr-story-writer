# Canon State, Events, and Narrative Time

## Purpose

Canon represents both persistent story state and accepted occurrence through
time.

The product must keep state and occurrence coherent without collapsing one into
the other.

## Canon Domains

Canon includes:

- **Character** — persistent canonical truths about people and person-like
  actors;
- **Setting** — persistent canonical truths about the world, locations,
  institutions, factions, cultures, technology, supernatural rules,
  geography, environments, and significant objects;
- **Events** — accepted occurrence through story history.

Character and Setting primarily represent canonical state.

Events represent canonical occurrence.

Both are authoritative within their respective roles.

## State-Event Coherence

An Event may establish or change Character or Setting state.

When acceptance or revision of an Event changes canonical state, the governed
operation must reconcile affected Character or Setting state as part of the
same semantic change.

The product must not knowingly leave accepted Event occurrence and accepted
current state in contradiction.

This does not require every state field to be mechanically derived from the
complete Event history.

## Historical and Current Truth

Canon may need to represent a truth that held previously and a different truth
that holds later.

Current state must not erase historical occurrence.

Historical occurrence must not be inferred solely from current state.

## Narrative Time

Story chronology and manuscript presentation order are distinct.

The product must permit chronological meaning to be represented independently
from the order in which the reader encounters events.

It must also permit the author to work on events in an order different from
either chronology or manuscript order.

## Temporal Precision

Story time may be exact, approximate, relative, partially ordered, unknown, or
intentionally ambiguous.

The product must not force false precision merely to satisfy a physical schema.

The exact temporal representation is a Planning decision guided by actual
product needs.

## Event Identity

Canon Events require stable identity when they participate in durable
relationships, chronology, state effects, later revision, or manuscript
mapping.

Array position alone is insufficient as durable Event identity.

## Promotion from Plot

Sequence represents intended occurrence.

Occurrence does not become Canon merely because it appears in Sequence,
Pseudo-prose, candidate manuscript, or generated text.

When the author accepts occurrence as canonical, the relevant Event meaning is
promoted into Canon and resulting state effects are reconciled.

## Canon Revision

The author may intentionally revise prior Canon, including prior Events.

Such revision follows DP-120 reconciliation semantics for affected Plot, Prose,
and Manuscript artifacts.
